## Week 1 - Project Understanding and Initial Planning

I started by understanding the purpose and scope of the CMMS project and the problems it aims to solve in the faculty-student mentorship process. I reviewed the initial discussions with the team and studied how the existing informal mentorship process could be converted into a structured digital workflow.

I contributed to the early planning by discussing the main users of the system, their expected interactions, and the major functions that would be required. I also helped organize the initial ideas so that the problem statement and proposed solution could be represented clearly in the project documentation.

## Week 2 - Requirement Analysis and Proposal Development

During this week, I worked alongside the team on reviewing the project requirements and understanding how they would translate into actual system functionality. I focused on the different responsibilities of students, faculty members, and department administrators and how their activities would connect within the platform.

I contributed to reviewing the project proposal and checking whether the proposed objectives and features were consistent with the problem identified earlier. I also participated in discussions about the system modules and helped relate the functional requirements to the later design and implementation stages.

This helped establish a clearer structure for the project before moving further into system modelling.

## Week 3 - System Modelling and Diagram Development

I took a more active role in the system-design phase this week. I worked with the team on developing and reviewing the UML use-case diagram and the data-flow diagrams for the proposed CMMS system.

I helped map the interactions between the three main actors, Student, Faculty, and Department Admin, and the functions they would perform within the system. I also worked on representing the major processes and information flows in the Level 0 and Level 1 DFDs.

A major part of my contribution was checking whether the diagrams accurately reflected the requirements discussed earlier. I compared the relationships between actors, processes, data stores, and flows and discussed changes with the team wherever something appeared unclear or inconsistent.

I also helped connect the system design with the planned modules such as allocation, scheduling, meeting notes, and reporting/dashboard functionality. This gave me a better understanding of how the diagrams would eventually guide the backend and database implementation.

## Week 4 - Diagram Validation, Database Planning and Implementation Preparation

This week, I continued working on the system design and took greater responsibility for validating the diagrams before implementation. I reviewed the UML use-case diagram and Level 0/Level 1 DFDs together rather than treating them as separate documents.

I checked that the actors and their actions in the use-case diagram were represented correctly in the DFDs, and that the processes, data stores, and data flows followed the same overall workflow. I participated in identifying ambiguous relationships and unnecessary or mismatched flows and helped discuss the corrections with the team.

I also contributed to the planning of the backend data structure by studying the information that would need to be maintained by the system. This included understanding entities such as users, assignments, meetings, and meeting notes and considering how these would relate to the processes shown in the DFDs.

Another part of my work was maintaining consistency between the project requirements, diagrams, and documentation. Whenever a design decision was changed, I helped check its effect on the other project materials so that different parts of the project did not describe conflicting behaviour.

I also coordinated with the team regarding the transition from system design to implementation. My focus was on making sure that the finalized diagrams provided a clear reference for the development work and that the team had a common understanding of the workflow before backend development progressed further.

By the end of this stage, my role had expanded from primarily understanding the project and assisting with documentation to actively contributing to system modelling, diagram validation, database planning, and coordination between the design and implementation phases.

## Week 5 - Frontend Implementation

This week I moved from the system-design and planning work into active frontend development, translating the use-case diagram and requirement discussions from earlier weeks into the actual React application structure.

I started by setting up the React single-page application and organizing the project into a component structure that reflected the three user roles identified in the use-case diagram, Student, Faculty, and Department Admin. I worked on scaffolding separate route groups and layout components for each role so that the dashboards could be developed independently while sharing common UI elements like navigation, headers, and reusable form components.

I contributed to building the core UI screens discussed during the requirement-analysis stage, including the student view for browsing and requesting mentors, the faculty view for managing office-hour slots and mentee rosters, and the initial admin view for monitoring department-level activity. I focused on keeping the component hierarchy consistent with the processes mapped out in the Level 0 and Level 1 DFDs, so that each screen aligned clearly with a specific system function rather than mixing unrelated actions together.

I also worked on the design system groundwork, establishing consistent styling, spacing, and reusable components (buttons, cards, form inputs, modals) so that the three dashboards would feel part of one unified platform rather than three disconnected interfaces. Alongside this, I paid attention to basic accessibility considerations, such as proper labeling on form fields and sufficient contrast in the UI, since this had been noted as a requirement earlier in the project.

Since the backend APIs were still being finalized in parallel, I worked with mock data and placeholder API calls structured to match the data entities discussed during database planning, users, assignments, meetings, and meeting notes, so that the frontend components would need minimal rework once they were connected to the real endpoints.

I coordinated with the team to make sure the screens under development matched the actor interactions defined in the use-case diagram, and flagged a few places where the original diagram didn't fully account for edge cases in the UI (such as what a student sees before being assigned a mentor). We discussed and resolved these gaps together, and I updated the relevant component logic accordingly.

By the end of this week, the foundational frontend structure was in place across all three role-based views, giving the team a working visual reference for the platform and a clear base to connect to the backend APIs in the coming weeks.
